#Q1
Read lengths: PacBio HiFi reads range from 62 to 46,056 bp. The Illumina RNA-seq reads are
101 bp paired-end reads.
Quality: The PacBio HiFi reads show good overall sequence quality. 
The variable read-length distribution is expected for long-read sequencing.
The RNA-seq data show good overall per-sequence quality, but FastQC reports reduced
per-base quality as well as sequence-content bias, duplication and overrepresented sequences.
Therefore, filtering/trimming with fastp is appropriate before downstream analysis.

#Q2
RNA-seq filtering and trimming:
fastp reduced the dataset from 45,241,360 to 39,807,212 reads, meaning that 5,434,148 reads (12.0%)
were filtered. Adapter sequences were trimmed from 3,510,455 reads. Read quality improved, with the
Q20 rate increasing from 88.9% to 93.8% and the Q30 rate from 78.6% to 83.6%. FastQC after trimming
showed that overrepresented sequences were no longer detected and the per-base sequence quality of 
read 2 improved from FAIL to PASS. Overall, the quality improved after fastp processing.

PacBio coverage:
The PacBio HiFi dataset contains 570,354 reads and approximately 9.90 Gb of sequence data.
Assuming an Arabidopsis thaliana genome size of approximately 135 Mb, the expected genome coverage 
is about 73×.

#Q3
GenomeScope output graphs: http://genomescope.org/genomescope2.0/analysis.php?code=I7GNN5lXW5JLtggInrp1
1. Is the estimated genome size expected?
Yes. GenomeScope estimated a haploid genome size of approximately 135.24–135.32 Mb for Lu-1. 
This is very close to the expected genome size of Arabidopsis thaliana. In the pan-genome study 
used for this course, the 69 accessions had assembly sizes between 128 and 148 Mb, with an average 
of approximately 135 Mb. Therefore, the estimated genome size is well within the expected range.

2. Is the percentage of heterozygosity expected?
Yes. GenomeScope estimated a heterozygosity of approximately 0.047–0.058% (model estimate ≈ 0.052%), 
which is very low. This is expected for A. thaliana, since it is predominantly self-fertilizing 
and natural accessions are usually highly homozygous. Population studies estimate that A. thaliana 
self-fertilizes approximately 97% of the time, strongly reducing heterozygosity within individuals.

3. Is the coverage expected?
Yes, broadly. GenomeScope estimated kmercov = 31.96. For a diploid genome, this corresponds to the 
single-copy/heterozygous k-mer coverage; the major homozygous peak is therefore expected at 
approximately 2 × 31.96 ≈ 64×, which is also visible in the k-mer spectrum. GenomeScope defines 
kmercov as the average k-mer coverage of the polyploid genome.
Independently, the PacBio HiFi dataset contained 9.90 Gb of sequence. Using a genome size of about 
135.3 Mb gives:
9.9 Gb / 135.3 = 73x
Thus, the ~64× homozygous k-mer peak is reasonably consistent with the ~73× coverage calculated 
directly from total sequenced bases. The values do not have to be identical because k-mer coverage 
is affected by sequencing errors, read ends, coverage variation, and repetitive sequence.

Bonus: Why are we using canonical k-mers?
Canonical k-mers are used because sequencing reads can originate from either strand of the DNA 
molecule. For each k-mer, the canonical representation treats the k-mer and its reverse complement
as the same sequence, typically by storing only one standardized representation. This prevents the 
same genomic sequence from being counted separately depending on strand orientation and produces a 
strand-independent k-mer spectrum. GenomeScope therefore explicitly recommends counting canonical 
k-mers with Jellyfish using the -C option.
